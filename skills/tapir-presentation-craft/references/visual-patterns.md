# Visual action patterns

Read this reference after assigning page roles. A pattern defines the visible
verb of a slide; it is not a finished visual style.

## Pattern contract

For every use, define:

- **subject** — the dominant object or phrase;
- **support** — the object or field it acts on;
- **anchor** — the exact shared axis, edge, aperture, path or target;
- **beats** — two to four perceptible states when motion is used;
- **result** — the static frame that proves the claim.

Use only one primary pattern per slide.

## Cut

Use for separation, prioritization, editing or a sharp decision.

- Anchor: one continuous cut axis.
- Motion: stroke arrives, crosses, then pieces visibly separate or hierarchy
  changes.
- Failure: a diagonal line floats over objects without affecting them.

## Pass

Use for transmission, flow, transformation through a boundary or context.

- Anchor: one continuous path through an aperture, gate or layer.
- Motion: object approaches, crosses, changes state, exits or lands.
- Failure: entry and exit paths do not align.

## Land

Use for selection, completion, token placement or answers arriving.

- Anchor: exact target slot or baseline.
- Motion: two to four rounds may repeat; later rounds can compress.
- Failure: the object stops near the target rather than occupying it.

## Focus

Use for attention, weighting, filtering or foregrounding.

- Anchor: one focal center or aperture.
- Motion: surrounding material loses weight while the subject gains contrast,
  scale or clarity.
- Failure: a magnifying-glass icon appears without changing the subject.

## Accumulate

Use for learning, evidence, iteration or compounding.

- Anchor: one stack, track, vessel or count.
- Motion: repeat the same arrival two to four times until the pattern is
  visible.
- Failure: several items appear simultaneously and are merely labeled
  “accumulation.”

### Odometer count-up

Use an odometer for one or two headline numbers that represent growth, scale,
accumulation, or a newly reached threshold:

- roll digits upward independently like a mechanical counter;
- move quickly at first, then decelerate into an exact firm lock;
- keep currency signs, decimals, separators, and units stationary;
- let lower-value positions settle first when stagger improves legibility;
- target roughly 1200–1800 ms and simulate 8–14 visible cycles rather than
  literally counting a large range;
- trigger on a presenter click or deliberate slide entry and reset on replay;
- show the exact final value immediately in reduced-motion and static states.

Reject continuous casino-like spinning, random digit noise, rolling every
statistic, or a counter whose spectacle competes with the claim.

## Unfold

Use for explanation, possibility, revealing structure or opening a section.

- Anchor: one hinge, edge, mask or expanding field.
- Motion: closed state, partial reveal, complete reveal.
- Failure: unrelated elements fade in from arbitrary directions.

## Lock

Use for resolution, commitment, fit or a final synthesis.

- Anchor: matched socket, contour, baseline or alignment point.
- Motion: approach, contact, firm stop.
- Failure: bounce or elastic overshoot weakens the sense of certainty.

## Replace

Use for before/after, model updates, reframing or a selected alternative.

- Anchor: one shared footprint.
- Motion: old state exits or is occluded as the new state occupies the same
  position.
- Failure: both states remain as disconnected cards.

## Direction and timing

Keep every particle, connector and impact effect consistent with the chosen
physical direction. Validate transformed silhouettes after rotation.

Suggested duration:

- single action: 480–900 ms;
- short causal sequence: 900–1600 ms;
- repeated pattern: 1600–4000 ms over two to four rounds.

The final static state must remain complete with motion disabled.
