# Page-role contracts

Read this reference when mapping a talk outline to reusable layouts.

Each role is a semantic contract. Use the named role in `data-role`; choose a
layout variant through `data-layout`.

## 1. Cover

**Job:** establish title, tone and one visual premise.

Required:

- one title;
- optional short subtitle or presenter line;
- one dominant visual premise.

Budget:

- title: one to three semantic lines;
- supporting text: no more than two short lines.

Avoid agendas, feature lists and decorative icon clusters.

Suggested patterns: pass, reveal, split, focus.

## 2. Intro

**Job:** create tension or a reason to listen.

Required:

- one assertion or question;
- one concrete human, product or situational cue.

Budget: one claim plus one short orientation line.

Suggested patterns: replace, focus, unfold.

## 3. Agenda

**Job:** show the route through the talk.

Required:

- two to five named stages;
- one directional path, sequence or progression.

Avoid independent cards with no route between them.

Suggested patterns: pass, accumulate, land.

## 4. Section

**Job:** create a decisive visual gear change.

Required:

- section number or label;
- short section claim.

Budget: fewer than twelve Chinese characters when practical.

Prefer a full-bleed color or material change. Do not add body copy.

Suggested patterns: cut, reveal, lock.

## 5. Statement

**Job:** let one claim occupy the room.

Required:

- one complete assertion;
- optional small source or qualifier.

The statement may become the visual itself through scale, crop or semantic
typography.

Suggested patterns: focus, cut, replace.

## 6. Relationship

**Job:** show one mechanism, action or transformation.

Required:

- one dominant subject;
- one supporting object;
- one visible verb connecting them.

Declare shared anchors for participating objects. Reject approximate
independent placement.

Suggested patterns: any one visual pattern, but do not combine unrelated verbs.

## 7. Evidence

**Job:** prove one claim with one case, image, number or source.

Required:

- evidence object;
- conclusion title;
- source or context when needed.

Budget: one evidence object, not a dashboard. Crop boldly without damaging
meaning.

Suggested patterns: focus, reveal, accumulate.

## 8. Comparison

**Job:** make one consequential difference visible.

Required:

- two comparable states on a shared baseline;
- one explicit dimension of difference.

Avoid symmetrical columns when the conclusion is asymmetric. Let weight,
distance, overlap or direction express the difference.

Suggested patterns: split, replace, align, diverge.

## 9. Summary

**Job:** compress the talk into a repeatable conclusion.

Required:

- one synthesis;
- optional two or three supporting phrases.

Do not repeat every agenda heading. Resolve the opening tension.

Suggested patterns: lock, accumulate, focus.

## 10. Q&A

**Job:** open the room while preserving the deck identity.

Required:

- direct Q&A typography or one decisive question;
- optional short invitation.

Prefer one clear color field or typographic signal. Do not stack a question
mark, circular symbol and Q&A label when they duplicate meaning or create an
accidental silhouette.

Suggested patterns: unfold, reveal, hold.

## Optional Thanks

Use only when the event benefits from a final hold after Q&A. Keep contact or
follow-up information sparse and readable.
